# Paper B: race-neutral outline (PI decision 2026-10-20)

**Decision (PI, 2026-10-20): the entire paper is race-neutral.** The question is what parents pass on through the
earnings IGE and why the IGE rises with the son's age; race is one dimension of heterogeneity, reported where the
components differ, not the organising question.

**Five headlines (PI, 2026-10-20)** - exact statements in EVIDENCE 11.28-11.40:
1. What is passed on: the split of the earnings IGE (0.396\*\*\*) into the parents' wage 19.0%\*\*\* (12.0%\*\* with the
   father's schooling in), hours 2.8%\*\*, stability 6.1%\*\*\* (13.5%\*\*\* at 45-49), place 0.4%; father's schooling
   12.9%\*\*\*, AFDC/TANF years 6.6%\*\*; rest 47.6%\*\*\*. Replicated: stability 7.6%\*\*\* (NLSY79 CYA), the father's
   schooling rising with age (NLSY79 youths); one-year parent reports carry nothing (NLSY97).
2. A childhood layoff costs the son more as he ages: 0.158 (25-29) -> -0.818\*\*\* (45-49); one layoff year -0.117\*\*\*
   at 45-49; within the same son -0.229\*\* per 10 years; in the wage; teens only; closures nothing (11.39).
3. Generous state UI in childhood weakens the stability channel: 9.3%\*\* vs 13.3%\*\*\* - a direction (difference
   p=0.496) until the sharper test (the father's own replacement rate at each layoff) is run.
4. The son's own search friction carries 31%\*\*\* of the IGE at 25-29 and 9%\*\*\* at 45-49.
5. Income tied to layoffs and unemployment passes on more strongly than other parental income (0.742\*\*\* vs 0.381\*\*\*,
   diff 0.361\*\*); income lost to plant closures does not (0.074, n.s.) - consistent with Shea (2000) (11.40).
