**Conditioning on the starting wage.** Columns (5)-(6) condition on the first wage of the pair. If that wage contains transitory error or a transitory component, growth is mechanically negatively related to it (regression to the mean), and conditioning on it also conditions on an outcome of parental income (sons of richer parents start higher), so the coefficient on parental income is then a within-starting-level comparison that can be biased in either direction. Column (7) uses the wage one interview earlier, which shares no transitory error with the growth outcome if the error is serially uncorrelated. Because sons of richer parents have higher wages and growth is strongly negatively related to the starting wage (the 'added control' rows), conditioning on the start RAISES the parental-income coefficient: catch-up after a low start or regression to the mean would work against, not for, the gradient. (5)-(6) mix this with the mechanical error term; (7) is the cleaner version.
