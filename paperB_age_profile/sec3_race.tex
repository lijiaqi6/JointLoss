\subsection{By race}

The rise is concentrated among White sons (Figure~\ref{fig:headline}). On the baseline sample the earnings IGE of
White sons climbs from 0.218$^{***}$ at 25--29 to 0.419$^{***}$ at 35--39 and 0.457$^{***}$ at 45--49; that of Black sons
is 0.242$^{***}$ at 25--29 and between 0.18 and 0.26 at every later age. The White--Black gap is $-0.024$ (0.040) at
25--29 and 0.272$^{***}$ (0.079) at 45--49 (Table~\ref{tab:ige_baseline}); on all sons with a measured parental income
its opening, the mean gap from 35 minus the mean gap below 35, is 0.232$^{***}$ (0.052) on bands 25--49.\footnote{Appendix
Tables~\ref{tab:ige_sons} and \ref{tab:agetests}. Code: \texttt{113.BaselineProfile.py}, \texttt{26.IGEByAge.py};
figure: \texttt{31.PaperBFigure.py}.} \citet{chetty2020race} measure rank--rank slopes of 0.28 for Black and 0.32 for
White children at 31--37 and expected ranks at the 25th parental percentile of 32.6 and 45.2; in the PSID these are
points on rank profiles that are flat in age for both races. The racial opening in logs is robust to cohorts, period
effects, attrition weights, an experience clock and a dollar-weighted elasticity, and with one year of parental income it
is about 0.09 log points in four public data sets, 0.19 corrected for the unreliability of that year (Appendix~\ref{app:race}).
Sections~\ref{sec:stability}--\ref{sec:daughters} ask why one component's share rises, and for
whom.
