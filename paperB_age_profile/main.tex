\title{What Parents Pass On: Wages, Spells and Skills in the\\ Intergenerational Elasticity of Earnings
\thanks{\footnotesize Preliminary draft. Each number in the text comes from the table or the code named next to it; the
tables are generated by \texttt{28.PaperBTables.py}, \texttt{44.PaperBReviewTables.py} and
\texttt{90.PaperBRaceNeutralTables.py}.}}
\author{Jiaqi Li \\ University of Sheffield}
\date{\today}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent The intergenerational elasticity of earnings bundles what parents pass on, and it rises with the age at
which sons are observed, from 0.28 at 25--29 to 0.49 at 40--44 in the PSID. An exact split of the elasticity by the
son's age shows that the parents' hourly wage carries a fifth of it, their spells out of work a sixteenth, their hours
and their state's labor market almost nothing, the father's schooling an eighth; where wealth is measured, parental
net worth carries three tenths. The split replicates in the NLSY79 children, whose mothers report every week of work,
and fails where the parents are measured once. The elasticity rises with age, within cohorts and net of calendar time,
because skill and schooling brought to the labor market pay more as sons age---the son's AFQT carries three fifths of
the rise in the NLSY79 and rises by the same factor in the NLSY97---and because a father's layoff during the son's
childhood costs the son more as he ages: nothing at 25--29, 11 log points per layoff year at 45--49, in the wage, within
the same son, and more than a layoff after the son is 20. Plant closures and national declines of the father's industry
carry no cost, and the costly layoffs are repeated ones with small income losses: the cost is unstable employment, not
lost income. The same layoffs pull daughters the other way: their wages rise with age, a difference from sons of 0.93
log points per unit share between 25--29 and 45--49 ($p = 0.001$), not explained by selection into work or marriage. Unemployment insurance at the father's wage does not
weaken it. The son's own spells carry three tenths of the elasticity at 25--29 and a tenth at 45--49. The rise is White:
Black fathers' layoffs cost their sons as much but are not concentrated in low-income families.
\end{abstract}
