\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

What parents pass on through the IGE of earnings is first their wage and their schooling, then their spells out of
work, and least their hours and their place; half of it is parental income that none of these measure. The
components differ in when they show. The parents' wage and the father's schooling show from the son's mid-thirties,
through skill and schooling that pay more as human capital accumulates on the job. A father's layoff in the son's teens
shows only after 40, in the son's wage, while a plant closure shows at no age. The son's own spells carry much of the
IGE in his twenties and little after. The IGE rises with age because the components that grow outweigh the one that
fades.

Each component implies a different policy and a different age at which to judge it. If the wage and schooling are
what is passed on, schooling and training act on it, and their effect on persistence appears after 35. If spells are,
insurance in the son's teens acts on it: generous unemployment insurance goes with a smaller spells component, in the
predicted direction but not yet precisely, and the father's own replacement rate at each layoff would sharpen the test.
If the group's labor market is, policy aimed at groups acts on it, but in these data it works only between races and
cannot be told apart from anything else that differs by race. Hours carry little, so incentives to work more are not
where the IGE is. In each case an evaluation that scores the son's earnings around 30 sees the early components and
misses the late ones.

The rise of the IGE with age is White, and the components locate the racial difference: in the father's schooling,
which pays off for White sons, and in who is laid off, not in what a layoff does. The Black IGE is carried by family
structure. What the half of the IGE that is parental income at equal measured components buys, and whether a layoff's
rising cost comes from what it does to the family or from what it reveals about the father, are the questions this
accounting leaves open.
