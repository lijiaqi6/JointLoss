# Evidence ledger for the structural papers on the IGE and public policy

Papers (2026-10-17): **Paper A** = joint job loss, family insurance and the IGE (not race-focused); **Paper B** = the age
profile of the IGE and why the racial gap diverges after 35. Sections are tagged.

Every result we have, positive, null or race-neutral, with its number, source, the model ingredient it disciplines
and its policy relevance. Goal (2026-10-17): a structural paper on intergenerational mobility and public policy, with
or without a race focus. Nothing is dropped because it does not explain the racial gap.

Model roles: **T** = target moment for estimation; **E** = external (first-stage) parameter; **V** = validation /
untargeted check; **M** = motivation / framing; **D** = design choice it rules in or out.
Estimates: coefficient (s.e.); \*\*\* p<0.01, \*\* p<0.05, \* p<0.1. B = Black, W = White.

*Note 2026-10-17 (`25.JointJobLoss.py`):* rows 3.16-3.22 are the publication version of 3.8-3.10. The joint job loss is
robust (future-displacement placebo null, closures, pre-trend controls, self-employment excluded) and race-general; the
race difference is design-dependent (equal in 23's sample, Black larger in the couple-FE event study). The excess starts
at k = -2 (dating between spouses' reports and/or shocks that reach the workplace first). Correction to
`paper/sec6_why.tex`: the joint losses do **not** explain why family income falls by more than the father's earnings
(18% of that gap; ~2% of the loss). Literature: joint displacements by the same plant event are known and routinely
dropped (Halla, Schmieder and Weber 2020: 3.47% of closure households; Marcus 2013: 7%; Banzhaf 2013: ~5%), and
Kawano, LaLumia, Ramnath and Stevens (2025, Labour Econ.) attribute the small US added-worker effect partly to correlated
unemployment; new here: the displacement-level event study, channels, race comparison, model targets and income role
(details and URLs in `output/joint_job_loss.md` section 7). Pending item `25` done.

*Comparison with the PSID (rows 3.8-3.10, 3.16-3.21):* the CPS confirms the PSID joint job loss with 19,881 husband
job-loss events in the same-month sample and shows it is concentrated in the month of his job loss (3.31), not a slow spillover;
ratios are larger in the CPS (10x same month, 5.5x at one year vs ~2x-2.6x in the PSID's annual / three-year windows)
because short windows strip out the baseline. Same-sector concentration (3.27 vs 3.9 / 3.20) and race equality (3.28 vs
3.8 diff 0.005) replicate. The PSID couple-FE race gap (3.17) does not appear in the CPS. Caveats: one household
respondent usually reports for both spouses (correlated reporting error possible); the CPS reason codes are not the
PSID displacement items (temporary layoffs dominate, 3.30); the panel follows couples for at most 4 consecutive months.
